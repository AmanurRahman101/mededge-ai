# MedEdge AI

MedEdge is a two-role clinical record demo. React and FastAPI are served together on Render. Neon stores accounts, consent, visits, readings and published notes. Private object storage retains report images and recordings. Resend delivers authentication emails. Meta processes consented images and voice. Shared demo accounts contain fictional examples; public clinical-production readiness is not claimed.

Doctors have independent practice workspaces. A doctor requests a patient connection; the patient explicitly approves or revokes chart access. An approved connection permits a doctor to start a consultation. Each visit has one doctor owner: only that doctor can add, edit, publish and complete its work while open. Other consented doctors can read completed visits without taking ownership of drafts. Revocation ends doctor access while published history remains in the patient's chart.

The doctor flow is: choose a patient, read concise health context and patient-reported medicines, start or resume a visit, add a prescription, report with voice or clinical note, review and publish, then complete the visit. New information after completion belongs to a new visit. Completion requires a published note or prescription and resolution of processing, privacy and draft checks. Empty visits and unpublished contributions can be explicitly discarded. Patients open dated visits and published contents, upload reports, review redaction and manage doctor access.

Canonical clinical records belong to the patient and retain their original visit association. An earlier report can be referenced by another visit without moving the original, duplicating its authorship or rewriting its date. Using an earlier report image with new doctor dictation creates a new contribution in the active visit. Records predating visits remain explicitly labeled as earlier records; the product does not guess historical visit dates or doctor ownership.

Report images and doctor voice are processed together into editable medicine rows and English/Bangla assessment text. Prescription images can be compared with the medication editor; phones offer Source and Review views. Doctors can also create a prescription from voice only or manual entry without an image, or create a visit note without a prescription. Original audio remains in private storage. The table separates medicine identity, strength and form from morning/noon/night amounts, custom schedules, instructions and duration. Unknown names, strengths or doses must remain uncertain or blank; explicit zero stays zero. The system must not invent a medicine identity or treatment dose to complete a table.

Doctors can type, add, remove and edit rows, inspect source evidence and search the Bangladesh medicine registry. Each row needs explicit confirmation; later edits invalidate it. Publication requires review of the doctor's note, both language versions, every medication row and the final review check. Drafts are saved explicitly with saved/unsaved status. Published revisions are immutable; corrections use amendments with fresh review. Printed prescriptions include patient, author, date and a bilingual medication table.

Medicine lookup uses the public DGHS-hosted DGDA registry through an authenticated same-origin search endpoint. Searches occur only on Search or Enter and are bounded and cached; no model calls or registry mirroring are involved. Results provide brand, raw composition, form, manufacturer and source identifiers. Selecting a reference helps fill medicine identity but never supplies a treatment dose or automatically replaces a prescription. Product-name search is supported; complete generic-to-brand search and pharmacy-stock claims are not supported. Manual entry remains available during registry outages.

The patient health summary is a plain-language English or Bangla narrative generated from published doctor history, including published prescription tables, and cached by input version. It requires no second patient verification step. Latest amendments supersede earlier text. Provisional OCR and unpublished drafts do not become summary evidence. Detailed source history remains separate. Past prescriptions are not automatically described as current medicines.

Patients maintain a separate current-medicines list with name/strength, optional instructions and confirmation date. It records patient-reported use rather than a new prescription. Patients can add, edit or remove entries; consented doctors can read but cannot rewrite the patient's report of use. List changes do not call a model.

The interface uses Geist, self-hosted Noto Sans Bengali, zinc light/dark themes and supplied pixel workspace/category marks. Patient navigation separates Home, Visits, Reports and Doctors. Doctor navigation leads to independent patient and visit work. The short overview leads into a consultation; source review, recording and publication stay in their task. FHIR and model trace controls are excluded. Patient language follows the browser initially and can be changed per device without rewriting clinical evidence, discarding forms or triggering processing.

Authentication uses one active secure cookie session per browser. Existing tabs pause when the active account changes. Draft revisions prevent stale saves. No credentials or clinical drafts belong in browser persistent storage or test screenshots. Browser journeys use fictional local SQLite fixtures and a fake model provider, with desktop/phone and light/dark verification. Backend coverage exercises consent, isolation, publication, completion, revocation, deletion, amendments and provider failures.
